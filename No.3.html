Abak ma Dabaq Biryani

A MacGyvered biryani for ten, built from what was already in the kitchen, for my favorite sister (I only have one).

Third post · Boarded October 7, 2026 · Serves 10

One · Service change

The post on art and abstraction is still coming. My position changed dramatically whilst writing, so it is undergoing "changes to the track". As penance, this is my contribution in the meantime.

This biryani came out of my sister’s request for biryani and my desire to avoid a grocery store trip for the necessary biryani spice pack, hence the name, an idiom for something MacGyvered. The depth of flavor comes from a yogurt marinade that sits for at least two hours, from onions fried to a light gold and no further, and from potatoes fried in that same onion oil.

This is the version I made at home. It is built on a spiced yogurt marinade, fried onions, mint and cilantro, and rice cooked in water seasoned with whole spices. The chicken is cooked before it goes into the pot, which makes the dish forgiving. And there are a lot of potatoes, because we like a lot of potatoes.

Most of the work is timing. Three things run in parallel. The chicken marinates, the rice soaks, and the vegetables fry.

Two · Ingredients
Three · Gathering

Before any cutting starts, pull everything out by where it lives. One trip to each spot is faster than hunting mid-recipe.

Four · Timeline

Start the marinade first and plan everything else backward from the moment it is done.

The rice goes into its soak with about an hour of marinating left, and the vegetables start with 30 minutes left. When the marinade is done, the chicken goes on and the rice water comes to a boil.

Five · Method

Set up

  1. 1Do a full mise en place. Measure the whole spices for the rice into one small bowl and the marinade spices into another. Lay out everything you need in the order you will use it.

Marinate the chicken

  1. 2Cut up the chicken.
  2. 3Put the pieces in a large bowl with 3 tbsp vinegar, then fill the bowl with water. Let it soak for 10 minutes.
  3. 4While the chicken soaks, dry roast the cloves, cardamom, peppercorns and fennel in a dry pan until fragrant. Grind them to a powder in a mortar and pestle or a spice grinder.
  4. 5Mix the yogurt with the ground blend, the powdered spices, the ginger garlic paste, the salt, the bay leaf and the juice of half the lime or lemon. Taste it. Raw marinade tastes sharper and spicier than the finished dish, so adjust with that in mind.
  5. 6Drain the chicken and turn it through the marinade. Marinate for at least 2 hours.
  6. 7Wash everything you have used so far. You will want an empty sink later.

Soak the rice

  1. 8About an hour before you start cooking, or a little earlier, wash the rice once and leave it to soak. Do this close to cooking time rather than at the start of the day.
  2. 9Set out your biggest pot for boiling the rice. The grains expand a lot.

Fry the onions and potatoes

  1. 10With 30 minutes of marinating left, prep the vegetables. Julienne the onions, cut the potatoes, halve the tomatoes, quarter the jalapeño, and chop the cilantro and mint.
  2. 11Fry the onions in vegetable oil until lightly golden, then take them out. They keep cooking after they leave the oil, so pull them early. This is the step that matters most. The onions flavor the chicken, and burnt onions put a bitter, burnt undertone through the whole pot. If they come out too pale, a minute or two under a low broiler or in an air fryer will deepen the color.
  3. 12Fry the potatoes in the same oil until cooked through, as you would for roast potatoes.
  4. 13Set aside one third of the onions for layering. The other two thirds go into the chicken.

Cook the chicken

  1. 14Bring the big pot of water to a boil with the rice spices and plenty of salt. Taste the water. It should taste like the sea.
  2. 15Pour about 1/2 cup of the frying oil into a heavy pot. Add the tomatoes skin side up, then flip them and cook until the skins slip and the flesh is soft enough to smash. Smash them and cook until they collapse into the oil. Let the heat do the work before you smash, not after. You can keep the skins in, as I do, or pick them out.
  3. 16Add the chicken with all of its marinade and the two thirds of the onions. Cover and cook.
  4. 17Turn the chicken once the underside is done. Turn it as few times as you can so the meat stays on the bone. Add the jalapeño and cook, covered, until the chicken is cooked through. Squeeze in a little lime juice and simmer for 5 more minutes.

Boil the rice

  1. 18Add the soaked, drained rice to the boiling water. Cook until it is about 70 percent done, with the grains lengthened but still firm at the center.
  2. 19Drain the rice, then fish out the bay leaves and the larger whole spices.
Transfer · Layering

The chicken goes in first, straight onto the oiled pot, and the rest builds up in two rounds. Split the chicken, potatoes and reserved onions in half before you start.

  1. 20Drizzle the bottom of the empty rice pot with a little extra virgin olive oil.
  2. 21Lay half the chicken and its sauce straight onto the oil, then half the potatoes, then half the reserved onions with the cilantro and mint.
  3. 22Spread most of the rice over that, then repeat with the rest of the chicken, potatoes, onions and herbs.
  4. 23Finish with a thin layer of rice.
  5. 24Cover with a tight lid and cook on low heat for about 20 minutes.
